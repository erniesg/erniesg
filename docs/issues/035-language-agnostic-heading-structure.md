# Detect headings from typography, layout, and ordinal sequence rather than word lists

depends-on: 012,024

## Provider

vm-codex

## Goal

Recover document structure — titles, sections, subsections, paragraphs — from evidence that holds for any paper, in any language, in any template. The current classifier decides most headings from hardcoded English keyword allowlists and arabic-or-single-letter numbering, so it silently drops the majority of sections on ordinary papers and glues each dropped heading onto the first sentence of its section.

## Observed failure

On a 12-page IEEE-style paper the pipeline emits **3 headings**: `I. INTRODUCTION`, `X. ACKNOWLEDGEMENTS`, and `REFERENCES`. Sections II through IX are classified `kind: body`, and six of them are merged into the following paragraph, so the reader sees `"II. COLLECTING A GITS EVALUATION SET While SWE-Agent evaluated their system on SWE-Bench, a collection of…"` as one run-on paragraph.

Two independent causes:

- Every ordinal pattern in the classifier is arabic (`\d+`) or a **single** capital letter (`[A-Z](?:\.\d+)*`). `I.` and `X.` match because they are one character; `II.` through `IX.` do not. That is the entire reason those two headings survived and the other eight did not.
- `namedSectionHeading` and `numberedSectionHeading` both require a member of a hardcoded English list (`abstract|introduction|methods?|results?|…`). A heading such as `III. PASSERINE: AN AGENT-BASED REPAIR SYSTEM` is in no list, so it can only pass through `fontOnlyHeading`, which requires `fontSize >= bodySize * 1.18`.

The failing headings satisfy neither font rule. Inspected at source-run level they are **faux small caps**: one line alternating 9.96pt capitals with 7.97pt small capitals in the same regular font, `bold: false` throughout, at the same nominal size as body text. The available evidence is typographic and positional, not lexical.

## Acceptance tests

- Heading acceptance never consults a natural-language word list. Removing every keyword allowlist from the classifier does not reduce heading recall on the regression corpus.
- Ordinal parsing is numeral-system agnostic. Arabic (`1`, `1.2.3`), upper and lower roman (`II`, `viii`), and letter (`A`, `B`) ordinals all produce a comparable ordinal value, and all feed the existing sequence machinery. A multi-character roman ordinal is recognised exactly as readily as a single-character one.
- Sequence evidence is required, not merely accepted: a run of candidate headings whose ordinals form a consistent progression is promoted together, and an isolated ordinal-looking line with no sequence and no typographic contrast is not promoted. Numbering that restarts per level is handled as a direct child rather than a broken sequence.
- Typographic contrast is computed from source runs and covers at least: larger size, heavier weight, small-caps construction (alternating cap/small-cap run sizes within one line), full-uppercase casing, and a distinct font family. Any one may serve as contrast; none is individually required. A body line with uniform run size and mixed case never qualifies on typography alone.
- Layout contrast is available as evidence: a heading line materially under-fills the column measure that its own body paragraphs fill.
- A recognised heading is never merged into the following paragraph. When a heading and its section body were built into one region, the heading line is split back out with its own provenance, and the paragraph begins at the following line.
- Levels come from ordinal depth where numbering exists and from relative typographic rank otherwise; absolute PDF font sizes are never copied into the output.
- Unnumbered headings (`Abstract`, `Acknowledgements`, a named section) are promoted on typographic and layout evidence alone, in any language, with no lexical test.
- Fixtures cover at least: arabic numbering, multi-character roman numbering, letter numbering, unnumbered small-caps headings, unnumbered bold headings, a two-column and a one-column layout, and a non-English document. Each is generated by the fixture script and recorded in the manifest.
- A heading that cannot be proved stays a paragraph. The classifier never invents a heading from position alone.

## TDD sequence

1. **Red:** add fixtures for roman-numbered small-caps headings and unnumbered non-English headings; preserve failures showing the sections classified as body and glued to their paragraphs.
2. **Green:** introduce one numeral-system-agnostic ordinal parser and one typographic-contrast evaluator, then route every existing heading rule through them. Delete the keyword allowlists rather than adding a roman branch beside them.
3. **Red then green:** add the heading/paragraph split so a recognised heading is never absorbed by its section body.
4. **Refactor:** collapse the remaining special-case predicates only after corpus recall and the full suite hold.

## Exact-head definition of done

- On the regression corpus, every numbered section in each fixture is emitted as a heading at the correct level, and no heading text remains inside a paragraph node.
- No keyword allowlist remains in the heading path.
- From a clean checkout of the immutable PR head, `scripts/agent-evidence --all` records `commit` equal to that head, `dirty: false`, and `result: passed`.

## Allowed secrets

None.

## Artifact outputs

Numeral-system-agnostic ordinal parsing, a typographic-contrast evaluator, a heading/paragraph split pass, and fixtures across numbering dialects and languages.

## Stop conditions

Stop before adding another keyword allowlist, adding a roman branch beside the existing arabic one instead of generalising, or promoting a heading from position alone.

## Human clarification protocol

If a heading carries no ordinal and no typographic contrast, confirm whether layout evidence alone may promote it; this issue assumes it may not.

## Recommended response

Route every heading rule through one ordinal parser and one contrast evaluator, then delete the keyword allowlists rather than extending them.

## Trade-offs

Sequence and typography evidence generalise across templates and languages; keyword lists do not and silently drop whole sections when a paper does not use the expected words.

## Free-form response

Losing eight of ten sections and gluing each lost heading onto its section body corrupts document structure more visibly than any single object failure.
