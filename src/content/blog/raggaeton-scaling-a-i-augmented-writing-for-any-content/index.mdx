## The Spark Behind RAGgaeton

Let's face it: the world is running out of data to train A.I. on. So then I had a thought – what if we could use A.I. to augment human generation of original content? As training data becomes scarcer, there continues to be a lack of content both quantitatively and qualitatively, certainly there is room for A.I. to do plenty of good in this space.

For me, there's always been this wide gulf between system 1 and system 2 thinking when it comes to *original* writing. I tend to have these draft outlines in my head, a rough sense of the argument I want to make. But the task of gathering and sanitising information, sitting down and typing them out into full sentences and paragraphs? They never get done. If only we have an army of research assistants at our disposal...

Enter **Large Language Models** (LLMs) powered by **Retrieval Augmented Generation** (RAG) to the rescue!

RAGgaeton harnesses the power of LLMs and RAG to bridge this gap, allowing us to produce original content at scale while staying grounded in our chosen sources of truth.

## What RAGgaeton Does

Not enough time to write? No problem! RAGgaeton is capable of automating content generation end-to-end:

1\. Comes up with research questions for you (you still need to seed the topic, of course)

2\. Does the research

3\. Generates headlines

4\. Drafts the full article

5\. Applies TextFX (rewrites with alliteration, unfolds ideas, etc.)

You can use RAGgaeton by seeding an initial idea, leave it to do its work then edit and revise the generated draft. In future iterations, I’m planning to implement an edit-friendly interface and make it such that you can write once and publish everywhere.

## Key Features and Benefits

I built it to be scalable for different content types and ways of chaining the different operations on content while leveraging state-of-the-art ColBERT retriever models (the researcher’s words, not mine). This ensures you fetch the most relevant chunks for your generation.

### Scalable Content Generation

Users will be able to define reusable content blocks once, then mix and match them to create any desired article type. Here’s a glimpse of how it works.

1.  First define a reusable content block in `content_blocks.json`
    

```json
  "Setting the Scene": {
    "description": "Introduce the setting with vivid descriptions to immerse the reader.",
    "details": {
      "optional": [
        "Time of Day",
        "Weather Conditions"
      ],
      "required": [
        "Location Description",
        "Atmosphere"
      ]
    }
  }
```

2.  Next, copy and edit a prompt like so that composes these content blocks into possible structures in `article_{article_type).yaml`
    

```yaml
generate_draft_travel:
  system_prompt: |
    You are an expert travel writer skilled in creating compelling and engaging content for online publications. Always respond with a structured, valid JSON, adhering strictly to the provided example format. Do not include any other text or explanations outside of the JSON structure.
  message_prompt: |
    Task: Given the following context, generate an updated structure for a Travel Article to be published.

    Context:
    - Headline: {headline}
    - Hook: {hook}
    - Thesis: {thesis}
    - Article Types: {article_type}
    - Topics: {topics}
    - Context: {context}
    - Data: {optional_params[data]}
    - Publication: {optional_params[publication]}
    - Country: {optional_params[country]}
    - Personas: {optional_params[personas]}
    - Desired Length: {optional_params[desired_length]} words
    - Scratchpad: {optional_params[scratchpad]}

    You may use the possible structures below as a starting point. Each structure is composed of content blocks. Rewrite, reorder, substitute, remove, add new or simply flesh out further details for the structure and content blocks so as to be coherent with your headline, hook and thesis.

    Please provide the response in the following structured JSON format:

    {{
      "draft_outlines": [
        {{
          "content_block": "Introduction",
          "details": "[Content Block Details]"
        }},
        {{
          "content_block": "Personal Circumstances",
          "details": "[Content Block Details]"
        }},
        {{
          "content_block": "Destination Overview",
```

3.  Just call the `generate_draft` endpoint with the relevant parameters and value for `article_type` and start generating!
    

P.S. We can algorithmically permutate the content blocks into structures rather than write them out, an area for future work perhaps.

### A.I.-Powered Research and Retrieval

RAGgaeton doesn't just write; it researches. It generates a series of keywords for further research on different platforms, performs the search and then persists the information to a database. By leveraging token-based late interaction mechanisms rather than document-level embeddings, RAGgaeton can find the most relevant information for your content, ensuring accuracy and depth. The current implementation supports `you.com` and `Obsidian vaults`, with more sources to come.

### TextFX for Creative Enhancement

Turn ordinary prose into engaging content with TextFX. Add alliteration, unfold complex ideas, explode a word into similar-sounding phrases and more – all with a simple `edit-content` command.

## The Tech Stack

For the tech-curious among you, RAGgaeton is built with:

1.  Claude 3.5 Sonnet and GPT-4o for natural language generation
    
2.  Supabase for database management
    
3.  Langfuse for tracing and monitoring
    
4.  Llama Index for efficient information retrieval (and subsequently, stateful chat with your documents and data!)
    
5.  Pydantic for data validation
    
6.  FastAPI for atomic API endpoints to expose `generate-research-questions`, `do-research`, `generate-headlines`, `generate-draft`, `generate-topic-sentences`, `generate-full-content`, and `edit-content`
    

This combination allows RAGgaeton to deliver high-quality, contextually relevant content while maintaining consistency and coherence.

### The Task

We asked different models to generate travel writing for the hike to Fu Gai Mountain based on this prompt for the vanilla LLM clients.

```text
write me a 600 word travel article about hiking to fu gai mountain in zhejiang
Scratchpad: I saw an ant try to move a flower on the hike, it was doing so with all its might. When not doing gradient descent, an A.I. engineer had lots of fun climbing a physical mountain. I took a challenging route and ended up climbing over rocks and caves.
```

Only the RAGgaeton models had access to the draft input notes above. So the outputs below are the results of variations on 2 dimensions: limited context vs. enriched context (with writer’s draft notes), zero-shot LLM generation vs. multi-step LLM generation.

## My Design Philosophy

In creating RAGgaeton, there are three key principles that I try to keep in mind:

1\. \*\***Portable data\*\***: Your content should be yours, accessible and usable across different platforms and tools.

2\. \*\***Tunable to your unique needs**\*\*: Users should be able to enjoy granular control over model behaviour, editing of outputs, operationalising the workflow, groundedness, etc.

3\. \*\***First-class support for Asian languages**\*\*: This is a personal passion of mine, given the current underrepresentation of languages like Mandarin on the web.

## Learnings and Challenges

Building RAGgaeton has been a journey filled with insights and challenges. Here are some of the key learnings:

### Abstraction vs Specificity

Balancing this trade-off is a constant challenge. I found it useful to start backwards from what I wanted to achieve by when, then decompose the end goal into individual elements, and see if any parts should be made more reusable.

This led me to split off `llm_handler.py`, `llm_processing.py`, and `prompts.py`. The `llm_handler.py` makes the call with the prompt formatted by `prompts.py`, and `llm_processing.py` parses the response to ensure it plays well with our endpoints. This way, every operation is atomic, and you can freely determine how to chain them.

### Constraining Probabilistic Models to Behave Deterministically

One challenge I faced was getting predictable behaviour from the language models. To address this, I used Pydantic to define strict schemas for the requests and model outputs. Here's an example of what can happen without such constraints:

![](./Screenshot-2024-06-20-at-6.13.05-PM-21719064449341.png)

Notice how `attraction` and `description` are nested within the `details` key in one case, and in the other, it contains `attraction_#` with `name` and `description` further nested within instead. These models are truly “creative”, so work needs to be invested in making them predictable and backwards-compatible with traditional programming paradigms.

### The Future of RAG

I think of prompt engineering, RAG, and fine-tuning as different tools within an A.I. Engineer's toolbox. Regardless of how context windows or other factors change, RAG is here to stay. Intuitively, it makes sense that the noise-to-signal ratio in context matters for performance. We're not yet at a point where models can tune out unnecessary information to zero in on the task at hand like world-class human experts do.

## What's Next for RAGgaeton?

I'm continuously working on improving RAGgaeton. Here are some of the next steps I'm excited about:

1\. \*\***Evals and citations**\*\*: I want to implement automated evals that alert me only when they fail, and a citation system that highlights which content chunks are on shakier ground.

2\. \*\***Refactoring and composability**\*\*: The Pydantic schemas are still a bit messy, so I hope to clean them up and avoid repetition.
